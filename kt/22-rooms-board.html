Rooms and the board

Four different kinds of many-person conversation exist in this app, each with its own packet type, its own encryption, and its own answer to "who can read this". Plus a fifth thing that is not a conversation at all: signed notices that outlive it.

In plain words

Explain it to a ten year old

There are four ways to talk to more than one person. Shouting in the street, where anyone nearby hears. A club with a secret password written on the invitation, where anyone who gets the invitation can join. A small named list of friends the organiser wrote down, where nobody can sneak in. And a room named after a place, so everyone in that part of town is in it. There is also a noticeboard, which is not talking at all: you pin something up, it stays for a few days after you have gone, and everyone can see who wrote it.

The public mesh room

#bluetooth is not really a channel. It is bitchat's single public room, which Airhop names as a channel so it lists beside the location cells. 

  packet type 0x02
  payload:  the message text as bare UTF-8 AND NOTHING ELSE
  flags:    SIGNED
  ttl:      randomly 5, 6 or 7

Why the payload has no framing at all

bitchat's BLEPublicMessageHandler reads String(data: packet.payload, encoding: .utf8) and takes the whole payload as the body. Anything wrapped around the text, a length prefix, a message ID, a JSON envelope, is displayed to their users as part of the message.

So the framing has to live entirely in the packet header. It works because both implementations derive the same content-stable identifier from sender, timestamp and content (bridgeStableID here, MeshMessageIdentity.stableID there), which is what the bridge keys on and what the Nostr copy dedupes against. The payload is also decoded strictly, matching bitchat-iOS: invalid UTF-8 is dropped rather than rendered as replacement characters.

PropertyDetail
ConfidentialityNone, by design. It is a public room. The signature stops forgery, not reading.
AuthorshipEd25519 signature, verified against the key pinned to the claimed sender. A missing key is a failed check, never a skipped one.
Display nameAlways suffixed #last4 of the peer ID, because a bitchat peer chooses its own nickname freely and Airhop renders what it announces.
Origin TTLDrawn from 5 to 7 rather than fixed at the maximum, so a passive listener cannot always tell "this radio authored it" from "this radio relayed it". Weakens the marker rather than removing it.
HistoryA 15 minute gossip window, so someone arriving late catches up on the recent room.
The bridgeOff by default. Turning it on republishes messages to a geohash-6 rendezvous cell on Nostr, so two out-of-range crowds share one room. Chapter 27.

This is the only room that carries media and live voice, and the reason is coherence rather than favouritism: attachments ride a signed-but-unencrypted path and never reach Nostr, so they only make sense where the content is public anyway and everyone present is reachable over Bluetooth.

Private channels

Airhop's own invention, and the room type designed for scale rather than secrecy of membership. 

  CREATION

     generate 32 random bytes = the channel key
     the key IS the channel's identity AND its read/write credential
     whoever holds it is a member

  INVITE

     airhop://channel/protest-medics?k=<base64url key>

     put that on a flyer as a QR code and a few hundred people
     join over the course of a day. No roster, no approval, no cap.

  SENDING

     seal( key, { msgId, senderID, senderNickname, text } )
       -> [nonce 24][ciphertext + tag]
     broadcast as packet type 0x50, ALSO signed at the packet layer

  RECEIVING

     THERE IS NO CHANNEL TAG ON THE WIRE.
     A receiver TRIAL-DECRYPTS with every key it holds.
     A non-member's attempt simply fails the auth tag.

Why there is no channel identifier in the packet

A sniffer cannot even tell which private channel a blob belongs to, or that two blobs belong to the same one. Trial decryption costs a few AEAD attempts per message, which is nothing, and it removes a metadata field that would otherwise let an observer map out which rooms exist and how busy each is.

Attribution inside the ciphertext

The sealed payload carries the sender's ID and nickname. Over BLE the packet is individually signed as well, so the sender is attributable at the packet layer. Over Nostr every member publishes under a shared channel-derived key, so the relay cannot tell members apart, which means attribution has to live inside the ciphertext. It is self-asserted among members, which is the same trust level as a nickname in any group chat. 

  deriveChannelNostrIdentity(key):
    HMAC-SHA256(key, "airhop-channel-nostr-v1" || counter)
    retry until it is a valid secp256k1 scalar

  Every member derives the SAME keypair, so they all publish under
  and subscribe to one author pubkey that is unguessable without the
  channel key and unlinkable to anyone's real identity.

The trade, stated plainly

One symmetric key, travelling in a link, that never rotates and cannot be revoked. Anyone who ever obtains it can read everything they can get hold of, forever, and there is no way to remove a member. There is no forward secrecy at all.

That is the price of unbounded membership with no roster and no approval step, and it is the right trade for the use case it exists for: a QR on a flyer that a few hundred strangers join through the day, where a roster would be impossible and an approval queue absurd. It is the wrong tool for four friends, and that is what groups are for.

It is listed as a gap in chapter 49, because it is stated less plainly in the product than the courier tag flaw is.

Reach is the creator's choice: Bluetooth only, or Bluetooth plus Nostr, where the same sealed blob is published so out-of-range members receive it. bitchat relays 0x50 without interpreting it, so the two coexist with no cost to either.

Private groups

bitchat compatible, and the opposite design: a fixed set of people rather than a place or a link. 

  CREATION

     groupID     16 random bytes
     epoch       starts at 0
     key         32 random bytes, the epoch key
     roster      up to 16 members, each:
                    fingerprint  = SHA-256(their Noise static pub key)
                    signingKey   = their Ed25519 public key
                    nickname
     creatorFingerprint = the creator's own fingerprint

     THE CREATOR SIGNS ALL OF IT:

       Ed25519 over  "bitchat-group-v1"
                     || groupID || epoch
                     || SHA256(key) || SHA256(roster) || SHA256(name)

  DELIVERY OF THE KEY

     the signed group state travels to EACH MEMBER INDIVIDUALLY,
     inside their existing NOISE SESSION, as payload type
     0x06 (invite) or 0x07 (key update).

     THERE IS NO LINK. Nobody can forward their way in.
     A member who is not reachable yet is owed the state, which is
     held in the group-invite outbox until they appear.

  A MESSAGE

     [groupID 16][epoch u32][nonce 12][ChaCha20-Poly1305 ciphertext]
     AAD = groupID || epoch

     group ID and epoch are IN THE CLEAR so relays can carry it.
     The inner payload is Ed25519-signed by the AUTHOR, so one member
     cannot forge another member's message.
RuleWhy
Bluetooth onlyGroup messages never bridge to Nostr, so a member who walks out of range stops receiving until they return.
Rotating the key bumps the epochOlder epochs are refused. This is how removal works: a new epoch key is delivered to everyone except the removed member.
The creator is pinnedA group keeps the creatorFingerprint it was created with, and a state naming a different creator is refused even at a higher epoch.
The epoch in the AADPrevents a message from one epoch being replayed into another: the tag would not verify.
No delivery receiptsNeither client has them for groups, so GroupSendResult reports whether the message was sealed and how many links it went out on, and that is the only honest signal the UI can give.

The eviction attack, and why check order is the fix

A group ID travels in the clear on every group message, so relays can carry it. Without the creator pin, anyone who saw one could craft a self-signed group state naming themselves creator, at a higher epoch, with a roster omitting the victim. The victim's client would treat it as a legitimate removal, destroy its own epoch key, and drop the room.

The defence is enforced in two places, because a group state can either update or delete a group. The store pins the creator for every write, and groupStateAction pins it before the removal branch, which never reaches the store at all. This is a case where the order of a check is the security property.

The 16 member cap is bitchat's, and it is what makes pairwise key delivery over Noise sessions simpler and cheaper than a tree-based scheme. Chapter 19 explains why MLS, which would be better at scale, cannot run on a mesh with no total order.

Location channels

Covered fully in chapter 23. In room terms:

Named by a placeMembership is being in a geohash cell, or teleporting into one.
Two transports at oncePacket type 0x51 over Bluetooth for anyone nearby with no internet, and Nostr kind 20000 for everyone else in the cell.
Its own packet typeBecause bitchat has the same channels but publishes them only to Nostr. Under 0x02 an Airhop location message would render in their mesh room, addressed to an audience its author never chose, on top of the Nostr copy they already receive.
PseudonymousEach cell gets its own derived secp256k1 identity, so posting in two cells is not linkable and neither is linkable to your durable identity.
No media, no voiceMedia cannot ride Nostr, so remote participants would never receive it.

Choosing between them

  WHO SHOULD BE ABLE TO READ THIS?

    anyone standing near me
        └─▶ #bluetooth            no encryption, media and voice work

    anyone I hand a link to, possibly hundreds
        └─▶ PRIVATE CHANNEL       one key, no cap, no removal, no FS

    exactly these named people, and nobody who was not invited
        └─▶ PRIVATE GROUP         signed roster, max 16, removable

    anyone in this part of the world
        └─▶ LOCATION CHANNEL      bridged to the internet, pseudonymous

    one person
        └─▶ DM                    Noise XX, plus the Double Ratchet

The distinction people get wrong

A private channel and a private group are both "private" and are almost opposites. A channel is a place with a password: the key spreads faster than anyone could add people by hand, which is a feature at a march and a liability among four friends. A group is a list of people: nobody joins by accident, and it caps at 16.

The right one-line test is: do you care who is in it, or only that outsiders are not? If who, use a group.

The bulletin board

The one feature that is not a conversation. Signed public notices designed to outlive chat: pin something to your mesh or your area for one to seven days, and it stays for whoever walks past an hour later.

What it is for

"Water station at the south entrance." "Medic tent moved to the car park." "Road closed, go around the back." These are not messages to a person, they are facts about a place that should still be there when the person who posted them has gone home. 

  PACKET TYPE 0x23, byte-identical to bitchat's BoardPackets.swift

  TLV, type u8, length u16 BE:

    0x01  kind              u8    0x01 post, 0x02 tombstone
    0x02  postID            16B   random
    0x03  geohash           UTF-8, "" = mesh-local board, max 12
    0x04  content           UTF-8, 1 to 512 bytes
    0x05  authorSigningKey  32B   Ed25519 public key
    0x06  authorNickname    UTF-8, max 64 bytes
    0x07  createdAt         u64 BE ms
    0x08  expiresAt         u64 BE ms, MAX 7 DAYS after createdAt
    0x09  flags             u8, bit 0 = URGENT
    0x0A  signature         64B   Ed25519
    0x0B  deletedAt         u64 BE ms          [tombstone only]

  THE SIGNATURE COVERS A CANONICAL, LENGTH-PREFIXED ENCODING,
  NOT THE TLV. So field reordering or padding cannot change what
  was signed.

  domains:  "bitchat-board-v1"      for posts
            "bitchat-board-del-v1"  for tombstones

Tombstones

Deleting a post is not deleting it, because copies are already on other people's phones. Instead the author publishes a tombstone: a signed deletion marker that only their key can produce.

RuleWhy
Only the author's key produces a valid tombstoneOtherwise anyone could delete anyone's notice, which is a censorship primitive.
A tombstone is retained until the deleted post's original expirySo the delete keeps outrunning stale copies of the post as they propagate through gossip. Retiring the tombstone early would let the post reappear.
An orphan tombstone, for a post never seen, is capped at the maximum post lifetimeWe cannot know the original expiry, so seven days is the safe bound.

How a post actually travels

  createBoardPost(content, geohash, expiry, urgent)
      │  sign over the canonical encoding
      ▼
  broadcast as packet type 0x23, ttl 7
      │
      ├─▶ every peer in range verifies the signature FIRST
      │      the store's ingest REQUIRES the caller to have verified
      │
      ├─▶ quota, expiry and dedup are checked in the STORE, which is
      │   the single chokepoint for radio, local echo and disk restore
      │
      └─▶ tracked by GOSSIP SYNC, bit 8, with a 7 DAY candidate window
             so a phone arriving tomorrow still gets it

  ON DISK: the raw signed wire bytes, re-verified and re-ingested
  on launch. Not a parsed object, so a corrupted row cannot smuggle
  an unverified post past the signature check.

Why the board is the best-designed small feature in the app

It is the only content type that is persistent, public, attributable, deletable by its author only, quota-bounded per author, and gossip-reconciled all at once. The signature covers a canonical encoding rather than the TLV, so reordering cannot change meaning. The store is the single chokepoint for every ingest path. The on-disk form is the signed bytes, so a restore re-verifies rather than trusting the file. And the tombstone retention rule shows somebody thought about propagation races rather than just about the happy path.

Two scopes exist. An empty geohash means the mesh-local board, visible to whoever is in Bluetooth range. A geohash scopes it to a place, so it appears for people in that cell. Urgent posts are flagged with bit 0 and surfaced differently. A notice only counts as "new" for the notification bell if it was created within the last five minutes, which keeps a channel's history replay and a gossip backfill of old-but-unexpired posts from flooding the bell.

Support is bitchat-iOS only. Android's MessageType registry does not include 0x23, so a bitchat-Android phone relays board posts without displaying them. That is the correct behaviour for an unknown type and costs nothing.

Moderation, such as it is

There is no protocol-level moderation, and there never will be

VISION.md states it directly: moderation is strictly client-side, and the project cannot and will not moderate content at the protocol level. The reason is structural rather than philosophical. A mechanism that removes content from other people's devices is a censorship mechanism, and building one into a censorship-resistant tool would defeat the tool.

One deliberate asymmetry worth understanding: ANNOUNCE is exempt from the block chokepoint. A blocked peer's announces still resolve transport-level routing, because blocking someone must not degrade the mesh for everyone else routing through them. onAnnounce keeps them out of the peer store instead, so they never appear in the list or the radar. Blocking says what you read, not who the mesh serves.

Drills

  • A private channel and a private group are both private. What is the actual difference? A channel is a place with a password: a single symmetric key travels inside the invite link, so anyone who receives the link can read, there is no roster, no member cap and no way to remove anybody. A group is a list of people: the creator signs a roster of up to 16 and delivers the epoch key to each member individually inside their Noise session, so there is no link to forward and nobody joins by accident. The test is whether you care who is in it or only that outsiders are not. Channels scale to hundreds and give up membership control; groups control membership and cap at 16.
  • Why does the #bluetooth payload contain nothing but the message text? Because bitchat's public message handler decodes the entire payload as the body, so any wrapper is displayed to their users as part of the message. The framing therefore has to live entirely in the packet header. It works because both implementations independently derive the same content-stable identifier from sender, timestamp and content, which is what the bridge keys on and what collapses the Bluetooth and Nostr copies of one message into a single bubble.
  • Explain the group eviction attack and its fix. A group ID travels in the clear on every group message so relays can carry it. Anyone who observed one could craft a self-signed group state naming themselves as creator, at a higher epoch, with a roster omitting the victim. The victim's client would read it as a legitimate removal, destroy its own epoch key and drop the room. The fix is pinning the creator fingerprint the group was created with and refusing any state naming a different creator even at a higher epoch, enforced in two places because a state can either update or delete: the store pins it for every write, and groupStateAction pins it before the removal branch, which never reaches the store.
  • Why is there no channel identifier in a private channel packet? So an observer cannot tell which channel a blob belongs to, or that two blobs belong to the same one. That removes a metadata field which would otherwise let somebody map out how many private rooms exist locally and how busy each is. The cost is that a receiver must trial-decrypt with every key it holds, which is a handful of AEAD attempts per message and effectively free, and a non-member's attempt simply fails the authentication tag.
  • How does deleting a bulletin board post work when copies are already on other phones? It does not delete, it publishes a tombstone: a signed deletion marker that only the author's key can produce, so nobody can delete anyone else's notice. Receiving devices remove the post and retain the tombstone until the deleted post's original expiry, so the delete keeps outrunning stale copies still propagating through gossip. A tombstone for a post never seen is an orphan, capped at the maximum seven day lifetime because the original expiry is unknowable, and orphans have their own quota so they cannot be used to fill the store.
  • Why is ANNOUNCE exempt from the block chokepoint? Because announces maintain relay topology, and blocking one person must not degrade the mesh for everyone else routing through them. Their announces still resolve links and peer state, so third-party delivery keeps working, but onAnnounce keeps a blocked peer out of the peer store entirely, so they never appear in the Mesh list or the radar. Everything carrying peer content drops at the single chokepoint in routePacket. Blocking says what you read, not who the mesh serves.
