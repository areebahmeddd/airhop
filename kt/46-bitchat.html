Airhop against bitchat

Airhop is a spiritual fork of bitchat, sharing its wire protocol byte for byte. Most "why is it like that" questions in this manual bottom out here. This chapter is the complete difference list, and the contract that governs what may and may not change.

In plain words

Explain it to a ten year old

Someone else built a way for phones to talk over Bluetooth without the internet, and published exactly how it works. Airhop speaks the same language, so an Airhop phone and one of their phones can talk to each other with no setup at all. Airhop then adds some words of its own, chosen so their phones politely pass them along without trying to understand them.

The compatibility contract

Principle 6, from VISION.md

"Airhop nodes must communicate with bitchat nodes. The BLE packet wire format, service UUIDs, and peer ID derivation algorithm are fixed. Breaking this requires a protocol version bump and explicit compat testing."

And principle 7: "When in doubt, do what bitchat does. The bitchat team are smart engineers who made deliberate choices."

Three implementations, not two

The distinction most documentation gets wrong

bitchat-iOS and bitchat-Android do not implement the same set of packet types. Android's MessageType enum carries 0x01 to 0x03, 0x10, 0x11, 0x20 to 0x22 and 0x29. Everything else is iOS only.

So "bitchat compatible" should be read as "bitchat-iOS compatible, ignored by bitchat-Android" for the courier envelope, board posts, prekey bundles, group messages, ping, pong and gateway carriers. Airhop treats iOS as canonical where the two disagree, because it is the more mature implementation.

Where Airhop deliberately diverges

BehaviourbitchatAirhopWhy
Outbox TTL 24 hours 7 days The one documented wire-adjacent divergence, and it is local: nothing about the outbox goes on the wire. Courier envelopes held by third parties still expire at 24 hours to match the wire rule, so the sender's own queue is the only thing that can still deliver on a later encounter. A week covers people who do not meet daily, which is the case this app exists for, and it costs no airtime because retries are bounded by the 8-attempt cap rather than by the clock.
Fanout Deterministic message-ID-seeded subset Full fanout Not a decision, a gap. PROTOCOLS.md documents subsetting; the code writes to every non-ingress link. Chapter 45.
Relay cancellation Cancels a scheduled relay when a duplicate arrives first Does not The timer map exists; the receive path drops duplicates before reaching cancellation. Wasted airtime in a dense room.
Sent photo cap 512 KiB received, ~45 KB in practice 256 KiB sent bitchat expires an assembly 30 seconds after the first fragment. 512 KiB is about 22 seconds of transfer, so it only lands if the link never forces a retry. 256 KiB leaves room for backoff.
Fragment data size 469 bytes 467 bytes Same 512-byte budget, slightly larger fragment header. Harmless: the chunk size is a sender-side choice and the receiver reassembles by index.
Gossip cache 1,000 on iOS, 100 on Android 1,000 Resolved toward iOS, as with every other divergence between the two upstreams.
Sync interval 15 s on iOS, 30 s on Android 15 s Same rule.
Announce freshness 900 s backwards only on iOS, 600 s symmetric on Android 900 s symmetric The union of what both accept, so no announce a bitchat device would accept is refused here. The forward bound is the cheap half and stops a peer parking a far-future timestamp.
Sync bit 7, files Serves files from sync Does not A 1 MiB attachment is tens of seconds of exclusive radio per asking peer, and bitchat's own reassembly often expires anyway. Symmetric and non-erroring in both directions.
Sync bit 9, prekeys Reconciles them Does not Not a decision, a gap. Costs forward secrecy on some courier envelopes. Chapter 13.
Source routes Originates and follows Follows only Originating requires emitting adjacency in cleartext, which bitchat's own peer-ID rotation analysis rejects, and which bitchat has since stopped doing.
Android background location ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION and a location service type neverForLocation on BLUETOOTH_SCAN Accurate here: a scan result is read for its UUID, peer ID and RSSI, none of which is a position. Asks for less.
iOS advertisement Service UUID alone Service UUID alone (now) Airhop used to advertise the peer ID in the local name. Removed: nothing read it, so it was a stable identifier broadcast to every passive scanner for nothing.
Tor default On, fail-closed (iOS) Off on both Turning Tor on used to be a visible signal in itself, which in several target situations is the risk. Airhop now ships obfs4 and Snowflake for exactly that, which bitchat does not. Chapter 26.
Android Tor Embeds Arti (ArtiTorManager.kt) Embeds Arti, plus obfs4 and Snowflake bridges Was a gap; now closed and then some. Airhop embeds Arti on both platforms from one Rust crate and ships pluggable transports, which bitchat does not. Chapters 25 and 26. Chapter 25.
Same-platform fast path on iOS Never had one Written, never worked on a device, removed Two independently fatal defects, and the 1 MiB cap means Bluetooth's ceiling is seconds rather than minutes.

What Airhop inherits, including the flaws

Inherited limitations that cannot be fixed unilaterally

  • Courier tag linkability. The HMAC key is the recipient's public Noise key, which every announce broadcasts, so anyone who heard one announce can compute that peer's tags for any day. bitchat documents the same flaw and notes its whitepaper's unlinkability claim does not hold. Fixing it needs a v2 tag keyed on a shared secret, agreed across implementations.
  • Nostr DMs have no forward secrecy. Static ECDH between two long-lived secp256k1 identities. Inherited from NIP-17 and from bitchat's cipher.
  • Sender and recipient IDs in the clear on every mesh packet. Who is talking to whom is not hidden.
  • Peer IDs do not rotate. bitchat has a whole design document on this and has not shipped it either.
  • No Tor bridges or pluggable transports on either platform, in either project.
  • The file size ceiling is iOS's alone. Android allows 10 MiB, iOS refuses past ~1.13 MiB silently, so there is no cross-platform number to raise to.
  • The 255 byte DM content cap, from a one-byte TLV length in PrivateMessagePacket, on all three implementations.

Naming these matters more than fixing them, because each is a place where a reasonable person would assume a stronger property than the system provides.

The interoperability failure list

Every one of these actually happened, and every one failed silently. This list is the strongest argument for keeping a bitchat actor in the test suite.

BugSymptomRoot cause
No handshake ever completedTotal failure to talk to bitchat, with no error.A skipped mixHash(prologue). Standard Noise runs it even when the prologue is empty. Airhop's handshake hash was one hash behind, and that hash is the AEAD associated data when the static key is sealed in message 2, so the tag mismatched.
Every courier envelope droppedStore-and-forward silently did nothing through bitchat carriers.Airhop stamped a 7-day expiry; bitchat refuses anything past 24 hours plus 1 hour of slack.
Every courier envelope unreadableSame, in the other direction: bitchat envelopes rendered a binary TLV as the message body.Airhop sealed bare UTF-8; bitchat requires the plaintext to be a typed Noise private message.

Signature verification would failValid packets rejected.DEFLATE is not canonical, and verification re-encodes and therefore re-compresses. Fixed by keeping the received wire form and reusing it verbatim.

The pattern is consistent: protocol mismatches fail silently. Neither side logs, neither side errors, and the feature simply does not work. That is why the conformance tests parse bitchat's own sources rather than restating their constants, and why the simulation includes a node written to bitchat's rules rather than Airhop's.

What bitchat does better

An honest list, because a fork that only lists its own advantages is not a review.

How to extend without breaking it

  1. New packet types at 0x52 or above. bitchat has reached 0x2C and allocates forward. conformance.test.ts fails if the gap closes to 16, so a future collision surfaces in CI rather than in the field.
  2. New TLV tags above the range the other side assigns. bitchat holds announce tags 0x01 to 0x06; Airhop's Nostr key is 0x07. Reading one as the other would corrupt both sides.
  3. New Noise payload types at 0x50 or above. bitchat holds 0x01 to 0x03, 0x06 to 0x09, 0x10 to 0x12 and 0x20 to 0x21. (0x22 predates the rule and stays where it is, because moving it would break every shipped build for no gain.)
  4. New sync bits well above bitchat's frontier. Their table ends at 10, so Airhop's channel bit is 24 rather than 11, and it must stay under 31 because 1 << 31 is negative in JavaScript.
  5. Never change a shared constant unilaterally. Every one of the silent failures above came from doing so, in good faith, for a defensible local reason.
  6. Add a conformance scenario. A new type should be proven to cross a mesh of bitchat phones while staying invisible to their users, which is exactly what X02 does.

Drills

  • Why is wire compatibility worth constraining the whole design? Because mesh range scales with user density, and the honest weakness of any phone mesh is that nobody else has it installed. Sharing bitchat's wire protocol doubles the effective user base at zero cost: an Airhop phone and a bitchat phone join the same mesh with no configuration, relay for each other, and exchange DMs. In a protest or a disaster, "how many people around me are running a compatible app" is the number that decides whether the product works at all.
  • Why did location channels need their own packet type? Because bitchat has the same location channels at the same geohash precisions but publishes them to Nostr and never over Bluetooth: on the mesh it has exactly one public room. So the Bluetooth copy is an Airhop-only path, and a bitchat peer already receives the Nostr copy. Sent under 0x02 it would render in their mesh room as well, addressed to an audience its author never chose. Under 0x51 it is a type neither client interprets, so they relay it without showing it, which is exactly the desired behaviour.
  • Give the strongest example of why protocol mismatches are dangerous. The 557-byte fragment. Airhop budgeted 469 bytes of payload rather than 512 bytes of frame, so every fragment exceeded the ATT ceiling. Android truncated the write and iOS refused it, the receiver's decoder failed on a signature whose last bytes never arrived, and nothing was logged at either end. Every attachment silently failed in both directions, while live voice kept working because a burst is capped at 210 bytes and never fragments, so the feature that looked most fragile was the one that was fine.
  • Name a place where Airhop is behind bitchat and say why it matters. Fanout subsetting. bitchat relays a broadcast to a message-ID-seeded subset of its links; Airhop writes to every non-ingress link. Airhop's own protocol document specifies the subset as a routing constant, and gossip sync already provides the repair mechanism that makes subsetting safe. Full fanout is fine at the 25 phones the simulation tests and is the most likely failure mode in a crowd of 200, where airtime rather than correctness is the constraint.
  • Somebody wants to raise the file size cap to 5 MiB. Explain the consequence. Sending would work, Android delivery would work, and the local UI would look fine, while every attachment to an iPhone silently stopped arriving. bitchat-iOS refuses any packet whose declared expanded size passes its framed ceiling of about 1.13 MiB, and it refuses it by returning nil with nothing logged. bitchat-Android allows 10 MiB, so the ceiling is iOS's alone and there is no cross-platform number to raise to. The conformance test reads that constant out of the vendored Swift source and fails if Airhop's caps stop fitting under it.
