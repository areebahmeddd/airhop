**Method.**

1. Each candidate finding was traced in the code, including the chaos cases: races, a kill at any `await`, a panic wipe mid-flight, a device transfer mid-flight and radio or network toggles. It was then compared with bitchat-ios and with the installed library source.
2. An independent review checked the first pass, corrected its claims where they were wrong and raised items of its own. Both are folded into the entries below.
3. The owner decided the open questions (section 4).
4. Every behavior fix carries a test that fails without it. Pure copy, comment and documentation changes are the exception.
5. Later passes walked the finished code again: a sanity audit screen by screen, each subsystem against its specification, every in-chat payload through the interruptions people actually meet, live and recorded media end to end, and a whole-system review. Their items continue each area's numbering.

**Statuses.** **Fixed**: the defect is closed. **Fixed in part**: the main defect is closed and the entry names what remains. **Accepted**: a deliberate trade-off (section 5). **Open**: not yet addressed (section 6). **Owner**: a setting or decision outside the repository.

**Threat model.** Anyone in radio range can transmit anything: forge any plaintext header field, replay captured packets, mint unlimited identities, and drop or corrupt what passes through them. They cannot break Ed25519, Noise XX, or SHA-256 preimage resistance.

**Ground rules applied to every fix.** bitchat-ios wire compatibility holds; Airhop-only payloads (the Double Ratchet, the in-session Nostr key, geo contact cards, private channels, device transfer) may change. No reinvention: existing helpers and installed libraries first, and an upstream report where the defect is upstream.

## 2. Executive summary

Airhop's cryptographic primitives and wire format match bitchat-ios: the Noise XX pattern and split, low-order point rejection, decode caps, the DEFLATE ratio, the freshness window and dedup. The serious defects sat in the state and wiring around them: a forged packet that could desynchronize the Double Ratchet for good, signing-key pins that did not survive a restart, courier mail that never opened between Airhop and bitchat-ios, a location-channel stranger folding into a friend's DM thread, a DM photo that could go out in the clear, iOS radio events that never reached JavaScript, mint errors that could cost paid-for ecash, live media that could saturate the Bluetooth mesh, and a release pipeline that would have built a same-named branch instead of the tag. Each is fixed and pinned by a test.

**Verdict.** Every Critical and High finding is fixed, except MED-4 (video decoders in a media-heavy thread), which needs a device to measure. Every Medium finding is fixed apart from SUP-5, a repository setting the owner holds, and two fixed in part, MED-17 and MED-18, which leave small media edges. Forward secrecy for internet DMs is the largest planned change (section 6).

**Attacks run against a live mesh.** Each row is an executable scenario in `src/__tests__/simulation/` (I04 in `src/services/__tests__/move-session.test.ts`), run against isolated copies of the app over a modeled radio. "Refused" means the app rejected it and told the user nothing false while refusing. Across every scenario the harness also asserts that nothing forged renders, nothing renders twice, delivery state never runs backwards and no sat is created or destroyed.

**Checked and sound.** The Noise XX token order, prologue and three-output split match bitchat-ios bit for bit. The ratchet root is seeded from the exporter secret through HKDF. `@noble/curves` 2.4.0 rejects the low-order X25519 points and a zero output. `sessionBindsTo` runs on both completion paths before a session is stored. The tie-break (lower peer ID initiates) matches bitchat-ios. The group creator is pinned. `keychain.ts` is the only `expo-secure-store` importer, every call passes `AFTER_FIRST_UNLOCK_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY`, and no secret is logged.

### 3.3 Payments

Rob Woodgate's review points on cashu-ts discussion #963 hold: short keyset IDs, per-unit restore, persisted swap previews for NUT-19 replay, seed derivation at first run, `selectProofsToSend` for melts, ambiguous swap timeouts, the melt timeout and swap-down before a melt; phantom balances and stale fees are closed by PAY-7 and PAY-13. A stored swap preview is cashu-ts's own `serializeSwapPreview` inside a version of ours, and P2PK inputs are signed once before storage. Mint errors are read by the NUT error registry: 11003 is outputs already signed (a reused counter), and 11002, 11004 and 20005 are pending. Melts are synchronous: a mint honoring `prefer_async` answers PENDING at once, which the melt flow would read as in doubt until a reconcile pass.

## 5. Limitations and accepted risks

| Item                                      | Behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Why it is accepted                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Ratchet desync past `MAX_SKIP`            | More than 1,000 lost DR messages in one chain leave a pair out of step until a restart, a LEAVE or the next rekey (24 h at most)                                                                                                                                                        | A replay raises the same error, so healing on it would bring back CRY-14's teardown. bitchat-ios makes the same trade                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| First ratchet message not retried         | The first Double Ratchet message of a session is not resent                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | The ratchet heals on the next one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

## 6. Open items

**Planned.**

- **Forward secrecy for internet DMs** (Medium). Internet DMs follow bitchat's NIP-17 format: sealed to long-term keys, unpadded, and kept by relays, so a key leaked later opens every captured internet DM. The plan seals Airhop-to-Airhop internet DMs to the one-time prekeys Airhop already publishes, padded, and keeps NIP-17 for bitchat.
- **Deniable ratchet messages** (Low). `DR_ENCRYPTED` carries a long-term Ed25519 signature, so a copy of the packet plus its message key proves who wrote it. The plan carries it inside the Noise transport, whose symmetric authentication rejects forgeries as cheaply and is deniable; Ed25519 stays everywhere else.
- **Prekey rotation** (CRY-6). bitchat-ios drops an unused one-time prekey after 30 days, safe there because its bundle keeps the date it was made. Airhop re-dates its bundle every 3 days, so a sender may seal to an old key for up to 7 days after the last bundle that listed it: a key must stop being listed at 30 days and stay openable for those 7 more, or offline mail sealed to it is lost.
- **Live video assembler byte bound** (MED-20, Low). Assembler maps are bounded by count, so four hostile signed streams could hold about 96 MiB on a fast link.
- **Reed-Solomon cross-implementation vector** for k > 1 (for example k = 3, r = 2 from bitchat), to lock the coefficient order.
- **Immutable releases** (SUP-5). They need a draft-then-publish restructure of the release workflow.

**Open findings.**

- **MED-4.** Play only video notes on screen, cap looping players at about two, and release players off screen, once a device shows the decoder ceiling.
- **MED-22.** Lower the video note resolution and bitrate (about 320 px at 150 kbps) once note sizes are measured.
- **SUP-17.** Pin nostr-tools' noble and scure dependencies to the root versions with an npm `overrides` entry, then run the Nostr suites and a bitchat Nostr exchange.
- **SUP-20.** Add `maxSdkVersion="30"` to `BLUETOOTH` and `BLUETOOTH_ADMIN`, drop `requestLegacyExternalStorage` and the expo-updates leftovers, and correct the mock's constants.
- **SUP-21.** Add `collectCoverageFrom` over `src/**/*.{ts,tsx}`, excluding tests and generated data, together with tests for the largest screens, so the public figure moves to the honest one in a single step.
