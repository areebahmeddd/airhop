**Method.**

1. Each candidate finding was traced in the code, chaos cases included (races, a kill at any `await`, a panic wipe or device transfer mid-flight, radio and network toggles), then compared with bitchat-ios and the installed library source.
2. An independent review checked the first pass, corrected its wrong claims and raised items of its own. Both are folded into the entries below.
3. The owner decided the open questions (section 4).
4. Every behavior fix carries a test that fails without it. Copy, comment and documentation changes are the exception.
5. Later passes walked the finished code again: a sanity audit screen by screen, each subsystem against its specification, every in-chat payload through the interruptions people meet, live and recorded media end to end, and a whole-system review. Their items continue each area's numbering.

**Statuses.** **Fixed**: the defect is closed. **Fixed in part**: the main defect is closed and the entry names what remains. **Accepted**: a deliberate trade-off (section 5). **Open**: not yet addressed; the entry says what closes it. **Owner**: a setting or decision outside the repository.

**Threat model.** Anyone in radio range can transmit anything: forge any plaintext header field, replay captured packets, mint unlimited identities, and drop or corrupt what passes through them. They cannot break Ed25519, Noise XX, or SHA-256 preimage resistance.

**Ground rules for every fix.** bitchat-ios wire compatibility holds; Airhop-only payloads (the ratchet, the in-session Nostr key, geo contact cards, private channels, device transfer) may change. Existing helpers and installed libraries come first, and a defect upstream gets an upstream report rather than a local reinvention.

## 2. Executive summary

Airhop's cryptographic primitives and wire format match bitchat-ios: the Noise XX pattern and split, low-order point rejection, decode caps, the DEFLATE ratio, the freshness window and dedup.

The serious defects sat in the state and wiring around them:

- a forged packet could desynchronize the Double Ratchet for good
- signing-key pins did not survive a restart
- courier mail never opened between Airhop and bitchat-ios
- a location-channel stranger could fold into a friend's DM thread
- a DM photo could go out in the clear
- iOS radio events never reached JavaScript
- mint errors could cost paid-for ecash
- live media could saturate the Bluetooth mesh
- the release pipeline would have built a same-named branch instead of the tag

Each is fixed and pinned by a test.

**Verdict.** Every Critical and High finding is fixed. Every Medium finding is fixed apart from three: SUP-5, a repository setting the owner holds, and two fixed in part. A relayed fragment refused by a full queue is still dropped (MED-17), and whether bitchat plays an iOS video note labeled `video/mp4` waits on a device check (MED-18).

**Attacks run against a live mesh.** Each row is an executable scenario in `src/__tests__/simulation/` (I04 in `src/services/__tests__/move-session.test.ts`), run against isolated copies of the app over a modeled radio. "Refused" means the app rejected it without telling the user anything false. Across every scenario the harness also asserts that nothing forged renders, nothing renders twice, delivery state never runs backwards and no sat is created or destroyed.

### 3.3 Payments

Rob Woodgate's review points on cashu-ts discussion #963 hold: short keyset IDs, per-unit restore, persisted swap previews for NUT-19 replay, seed derivation at first run, `selectProofsToSend` for melts, ambiguous swap timeouts, the melt timeout and swap-down before a melt. Phantom balances and stale fees are closed by PAY-7 and PAY-13.

A stored swap preview is cashu-ts's own `serializeSwapPreview` output, wrapped in a version number of ours. P2PK inputs are signed once, before storage. Mint errors are read by the NUT error registry: 11003 means outputs already signed (a reused counter), and 11002, 11004 and 20005 mean pending. Melts are synchronous: a mint that honors `prefer_async` answers PENDING at once, which the melt flow would read as in doubt until a reconcile pass.

Only `MainActivity`, the boot receiver and the widget receiver are exported, and the widget redraws from private preferences alone. PendingIntents are immutable. Backup and device-transfer extraction exclude everything, and cleartext traffic is blocked. On-disk attachment names cannot traverse. Diagnostics exports hold no content, names, keys or peer IDs. The iOS Aware listener admits only paired devices. No routing or crypto decision was found in Swift or Kotlin: the native layer carries raw bytes only.

### 3.6 Live and recorded media

The live video and push-to-talk core matches bitchat-ios constant for constant (packet format, Reed-Solomon polynomial and coefficients, packetizer, assembler, pacer, congestion controller, presets and caps), and its golden vectors pass. The receiver adds two bounds bitchat-ios lacks: a byte budget, and an expiry for units that never finish.

The findings sat in the radio layer underneath, which has to serialize, prioritize and age out live packets, and in playback and lifecycle edges.

## 7. Future work

Work deliberately not built yet, ordered by what it buys a user. **Breaking** says who has to change: no one; Airhop only (new behavior between Airhop phones, gated on capability bit 26, bitchat unaffected); or bitchat-wide (works only if bitchat moves too, since going alone would split the network). **Size** is S for days, M for one to three weeks, L for one to three months, XL when bitchat has to move, and External when it takes money and an outside team.

| Item                                            | What it buys                                                                              | Breaking     | Size     |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | -------- |
| Independent audits                              | Outside proof the crypto and the mesh are right                                           | None         | External |
| Reproducible Android builds                     | Anyone can check the APK matches the source                                               | None         | M        |
| Forward secrecy for groups and private channels | A leaked room key doesn't open the room's history                                         | Airhop only  | L        |
| Post-quantum key agreement                      | Recorded traffic stays safe from a future quantum attack                                  | Airhop only  | M        |
| High-risk preset                                | Safe settings in one tap for the people who need them                                     | None         | S to M   |
| Passphrase mode for data at rest                | A seized, locked phone gives up nothing                                                   | None         | M to L   |
| LAN reach across restarts                       | Friends on one venue Wi-Fi reconnect after a restart without meeting over Bluetooth first | None         | M        |
| Rotating radio IDs                              | No tracking a phone across places by its radio ID                                         | bitchat-wide | XL       |
| Multiple devices per identity                   | One identity on a phone and a tablet                                                      | bitchat-wide | XL       |

- **Independent audits.** Cryptographic correctness can't be self-certified, so Airhop shouldn't be recommended for high-risk use until someone outside has checked it, as Signal, [Threema](https://threema.com/en/blog/ibex), Briar, SimpleX and Session have been. Two audits: the cryptography (Noise XX, the Double Ratchet, courier seals, prekeys and sealed internet DMs, packet signing, the keychain, the panic wipe, device transfer, the wallet), and the mesh with the Nostr bridge and Tor. Apply to the [OTF Security Lab](https://www.opentech.fund/labs/security-lab/) or hire a firm, and publish the reports in full.
- **Reproducible Android builds.** The Tor client and pluggable transports are reproducible and hash-pinned and the APK has signed provenance, but the APK itself can't be rebuilt byte for byte, as [Signal's and Briar's can](https://reproducible-builds.org/). Pin the whole toolchain in a container, strip timestamps and machine paths from the JS bundle and native libraries, check two clean builds match, then publish through [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/docs/Reproducible_Builds/), which also reaches people without the Play Store. No one can reproduce iOS builds.
- **Forward secrecy for groups and private channels.** Anyone who later gets a group's epoch key reads every message recorded under it, and a private channel's key rides in its invite link and never changes. For groups whose members all run Airhop, give each member a sender-key chain that ratchets per message and restarts each epoch, delivered over Noise as epoch keys are. Let a channel's creator rotate its key and hand out a fresh link.
- **Post-quantum key agreement.** Ciphertext kept on relays (internet DMs, courier drops) can be recorded now and broken later; Signal added [PQXDH](https://signal.org/docs/specifications/pqxdh/) and [SPQR](https://signal.org/blog/spqr/) for this. Between Airhop phones, run an [ML-KEM-768](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) exchange inside the Noise session and mix its secret into the ratchet seed, about 2.3 KB once per session.
- **High-risk preset.** The defaults favor reach, so someone at a protest or a border has to find each switch alone. One switch, at onboarding and in Settings: Tor on (or the internet off), Wi-Fi Aware and the local network off, the app lock on with no delay, and 1-day disappearing messages for new chats. Show what it changed and leave each setting adjustable.
- **Passphrase mode for data at rest.** Keys are readable after the first unlock since boot ([data protection classes](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/data-protection-classes-secb010e978a/web)), which iOS needs to relaunch Airhop for a Bluetooth event, so a phone seized while on is at its weakest. An optional mode would encrypt history under a memory-hard key from a passphrase and keep keychain items readable only while unlocked. The cost: the mesh stops receiving and relaying while the phone is locked, and every store opens asynchronously.
- **LAN reach across restarts.** A peer's LAN address lives with the session, so after an app restart (frequent in the iOS background) two phones on a network that filters mDNS stay on Bluetooth until they meet again. Briar keeps its contacts' addresses and redials. Keep each saved contact's last address and this phone's own listening port, and after a restart let either side dial a stored address under the same rules as a fresh one, since the side that restarted is the one whose old address went stale. Worth it only once direct dial has been proven on a real venue network, and only where no Bluetooth path rebuilds the session anyway.
- **Rotating radio IDs.** Every packet carries a peer ID derived from the long-term key, so a Bluetooth sniffer can follow a phone across places and days. Peer ID derivation is fixed (AGENTS.md), and gossip sync and DM addressing depend on it, so this waits for bitchat, whose own whitepaper lists it as future work.
- **Multiple devices per identity.** Device transfer moves an identity and never copies it. The peer ID is one device's key, and prekeys and sessions are per device, so this needs a new identity model shared with bitchat.
